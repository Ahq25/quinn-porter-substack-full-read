# The Thing Before the Thing

*Exaptation, Coherence, and the Reuse of What Already Exists*

Quinn Porter

A ripple reveals the water. A loop reveals the rule the ants were following. An insight reveals a relationship that had been participating in thought before it could be recognized as a whole.

But recognition is not always the end of the story.

Once a relationship holds together, it can become available for something else. A structure that arose under one set of conditions can meet a different world and take on a different role. What once served one purpose may later make another purpose possible. Sometimes a structure that did not serve any particular purpose becomes useful only after the conditions around it change.

The earlier structure did not need to anticipate its later use.

That is the next part of the same story.

## Darwin's Lesson: An Old Structure, a New Use

In *On the Origin of Species*, Darwin considered the joints between the skull bones of young mammals. These joints, called sutures, allow some movement between the bones. They can help the skull accommodate the passage through birth.

It would be easy to look at that usefulness and imagine the skull had developed its joints *for* this purpose.

But young birds and reptiles also have sutures between their skull bones, even though they hatch from eggs. The structure did not have to originate in response to the demands of mammalian birth. Something already present could later become useful under a new set of conditions.

The difference matters. The history of a structure and the history of its present function are not necessarily the same history.

Darwin was describing a possibility that evolutionary biologists Stephen Jay Gould and Elisabeth Vrba would later name **exaptation**: the recruitment of an existing feature for a role different from the one, if any, for which it originally arose.

The later usefulness is real. So is the earlier history. What need not be real is the appearance that the earlier history was aiming toward the later result.

The structure came first. A use came later.

## Feathers Before Flight

Consider a feather.

Feathers existed among dinosaurs before the evolution of powered flight. Early feathers could help retain warmth or contribute to display. They were already part of living bodies, doing work in the circumstances in which those bodies existed.

Over evolutionary time, feathers also became part of the equipment of flight. Their shape, arrangement, and interaction with moving air made a new use possible.

From the perspective of a flying bird, feathers seem inseparable from flight. It is tempting to read that final role backward into the beginning, as though the first feather were an unfinished wing.

But a feather does not need to be an unfinished wing to have a history worth preserving. It only needs to arise, persist, and take part in what its surroundings allow it to do. Later conditions may bring forward a capacity that was not its earlier function.

The bones of the mammalian middle ear tell a related story. Parts of the jaw apparatus in distant ancestors were transformed, over many generations, into structures involved in transmitting sound. What had participated in one arrangement came to participate in another.

The result is new, but it is not made from an entirely new world.

Older structures carry possibilities into conditions that did not exist when those structures first appeared.

## The Thing Before the Thing

The final arrangement changes how its earlier pieces look.

After flight exists, feathers look like preparations for flight. After a hearing mechanism exists, its ancestral bones seem to have been waiting for sound. After an insight arrives, an earlier remark, a failed attempt, or an overlooked clue can appear to have been pointing toward the answer all along.

This is the thing before the thing.

A part of the later outcome was present earlier, but not as the completed outcome. It was present as a structure capable of entering other relationships.

That distinction separates a history that makes something possible from a history that already contains its destination.

The earlier feather was not secretly a finished wing. The earlier clue was not secretly a finished explanation. Both could matter to a result that had not yet formed.

Once the new arrangement exists, however, its contributing parts can be seen in relation to it. The past becomes easier to read because the present provides a whole in which its pieces now have a place.

The sense of anticipation arises in hindsight. The order of events remains unchanged.

## What Must Last for Something to Be Reused

For an earlier structure to take part in a later arrangement, something about it must remain available.

A footprint washed away before the next traveler arrives cannot guide that traveler. A trail that remains can alter the next journey. A connection forgotten before it can meet another idea cannot participate in their union. A relationship that endures, even without being fully understood, can affect what becomes possible later.

This is where coherence enters the picture.

Coherence concerns the ability of an organization to hold together through ongoing change. A living body maintains its organization while exchanging matter and energy with its surroundings. A repeated path can remain available because new movement reinforces it. A learned relationship can continue influencing thought long after the event in which it was formed.

What persists is not necessarily a frozen object. Sometimes it is a maintained pattern: an arrangement renewed by activity, carried forward through each encounter.

That persistence gives the past a way to participate in the present.

A retained structure can become part of a new arrangement because it has not been lost before the opportunity arrives. Some relationships endure long enough to meet other relationships. Some can then be joined, modified, or used in circumstances unlike those in which they first formed.

Persistence makes reuse possible. It does not guarantee reuse, and it does not decide in advance what the new role will be.

## Why the Death Spiral Still Matters

The ant death spiral offers an important warning here.

Its circular trail is coherent in the ordinary sense that each passage reinforces the next. The ants maintain a recognizable pattern, and the pattern constrains the ants that maintain it.

Yet the loop can be disastrous.

The spiral shows that a strong, self-reinforcing organization is not automatically a useful one. Its persistence does not guarantee a good relationship with food, shelter, changing conditions, or the survival of the colony.

Exaptation brings another question into view: **what can an existing structure do when it enters a new relationship?**

A feather that helps an animal fly has acquired a use in its wider circumstances. A retained idea that helps solve a new problem has acquired a use in a wider understanding. A closed trail that keeps ants circling may maintain itself while losing contact with what the colony needs.

The ability to hold together and the ability to serve a new situation are different things.

That is why the ant spiral remains a useful companion to this account. It reveals how an organization can renew itself. Exaptation concerns what may happen when retained organization is recruited into a different arrangement.

## When Meaning Arrives Later

A familiar sentence can be read for years without its deeper significance becoming apparent. The words remain in the same order. Their sounds and shapes do not change. Then a new experience supplies a relationship that was missing, and the sentence becomes intelligible in a different way.

The words have not been rebuilt. Their place within understanding has changed.

The same can happen with an observation. A detail first noticed for one reason may become important to an entirely different question. A failed solution may leave behind a useful distinction. A comparison made casually may later become the organizing relation of an insight.

Earlier events leave consequences. Some consequences continue shaping what can be recognized, expected, or attempted. They may influence thought without being available together as an explicit answer.

Then a new arrangement forms, and several earlier relationships become mutually intelligible.

The click is the recognition of that new whole. The history that contributed to it is older than the click, but the whole itself is new.

This explains why understanding can feel discovered rather than assembled. The contributing material was not invented at the instant of recognition. Yet it would be a mistake to imagine that a complete answer was waiting unchanged behind a curtain.

What was already there was a history of participating relations.

What became available was their new organization.

## When a Whole Becomes a Piece

An insight can change more than the problem it solves.

Imagine struggling through several examples until a common relationship suddenly becomes clear. The examples no longer stand apart. One understanding connects them.

Later, a different problem appears. That earlier understanding can now be used without rebuilding it from every original example. What first arrived as a whole has become a piece of something larger.

The process can continue. A connection helps form an insight. The insight helps organize a wider understanding. That understanding changes which details will be noticed when another question arrives.

This is a form of **active inheritance**: an earlier organization continues to influence what happens next. The past is carried forward not merely as a record, but as a usable participant.

Biological exaptation and the reuse of understanding are not identical mechanisms. Evolutionary features change across generations through inheritance, variation, and selection. Ideas are retained and reworked within learning, memory, language, and social life.

But they illuminate a shared organizational relationship: a structure formed in one setting can endure long enough to participate in another, gaining a role that its earlier circumstances did not require.

An insight is especially interesting because that change can be experienced from within.

## Reuse Within Continuing Experience

The still pool offers a way to keep two events distinct.

The water is already present before the pebble falls. The disturbance changes what becomes perceptible on its surface; it does not bring the water into existence.

Likewise, a new understanding occurs within experience that is already underway. A person can wonder, remember, compare, and remain aware of an unresolved question before the answer takes shape.

At insight, a particular separation in thought changes. Relations that could not be used together become available as one understanding. Once that understanding persists, it may be carried into a later problem and given another use.

The new relationship does not have to create a new consciousness in order to matter.

Its importance is that it has become part of what an already experiencing system can carry forward.

Outside conscious life, an evolutionary feature can acquire a new role without anything recognizing that change. Inside conscious life, the reuse of an earlier relation may be felt as discovery. The two cases are connected here by the history and reuse of organization, not by a claim that biological evolution and subjective insight are literally the same process.

The distinction preserves what each example teaches.

## Why the Past Can Seem to Know the Future

When a solution finally becomes clear, the path toward it can seem almost intentional.

A remark that once sounded incidental now appears essential. An earlier mistake reveals the distinction it forced into view. A forgotten example turns out to be exactly the example that makes the answer understandable.

It is easy to say that the pieces were leading toward the solution.

In one sense, they were. They shaped the possibilities available to later thought. They influenced what could be remembered, compared, ruled out, or retained.

But that influence is different from foresight.

Earlier structures constrain what can come next without containing an image of everything that will follow. Many retained patterns never gain a new use. Others become useful only because circumstances change in ways that could not have been predicted from their origin.

The new whole makes the earlier path legible. It does not reach backward and direct the path.

An evolutionary structure may seem made for the task it now performs. A sentence may seem written for the understanding it later awakens. An insight may make its own preparation look inevitable.

In each case, the later organization changes what can be seen in the earlier history.

It does not change what happened.

## The Past That Remains Available

The pond shows how an ongoing medium can become noticeable.

The death spiral shows how a distributed rule can become concentrated in a recognizable pattern, even when the result is harmful.

Exaptation adds a further possibility. A retained structure can meet new conditions and become part of an organization that uses it differently.

These are not three copies of one mechanism. They reveal related aspects of how organization persists, becomes recognizable, and acquires consequences beyond the moment of its formation.

What matters about an earlier structure is not only what it did when it first appeared. It is also what its persistence makes available to what comes later.

A feather can take part in flight. An ancestral bone can take part in hearing. An old sentence can become newly meaningful. An insight can become one piece of another insight.

The later use does not erase the earlier history. It depends on it.

The thing before the thing was never required to know what it would become.

It only had to remain capable of taking part in what came next.
